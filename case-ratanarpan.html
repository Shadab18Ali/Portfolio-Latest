Overview

A gifting brand with festival-driven demand and a small team.

The brand needed a complete online store and a steady stream of festival-timed content. I built the store, then designed the scheduled tasks that plan, draft and maintain that content.

The Challenge

Festive and corporate gifting runs on deadlines. Each festival needs banners, featured products and blog content published on time, and the team was too small to brief, write, schedule and optimise all of it by hand.

Constraints

  • People keep the creative and editorial decisions. The design team makes every banner.
  • Nothing publishes without a chance for the team to edit or stop it.
  • New content must not duplicate what is already on the blog.

What I Built

  • The Shopify store: theme, homepage sections for collections, festive campaigns, bestsellers, the gifting process, corporate gifts and custom hampers, and every product listing, created through Claude's Shopify connector.
  • Scheduled tasks that request banners from the design team with a matched in-stock product, send deadline reminders and escalate overdue banners.
  • A blog workflow that turns banner emails and a festival and evergreen calendar into SEO drafts with a target length of 800–1,200 words and product placements, checked for duplicates.
  • Weekly SEO upkeep: meta titles and descriptions refreshed from new keyword research, product images renamed, and a check that re-runs any missed task.

Key Improvements

Campaign planning

A festival and evergreen calendar sets what to publish and when.

Banner request

Scheduled tasks ask the design team for each banner, with a matched in-stock product, reminders and escalation.

AI-assisted draft

The banner email and calendar entry become an SEO draft with product placements, checked against existing posts for duplicates.

Human review

Every draft waits 24 hours before it publishes, so the team can edit or stop it.

Publish and maintain

The post goes live on the store's blog. Weekly tasks refresh SEO metadata and re-run anything missed.

The Key Decision

Automate the routine work (briefs, drafts, scheduling and SEO upkeep) but keep people on the creative and editorial decisions. The safeguards make that practical: duplicate checks on every draft, a 24-hour review window before publishing, and a check that re-runs any task that was missed.

Results

  • A small team publishes on time for every festival.
  • People stay in charge of design and have a full day to review each post.
  • Meta titles, descriptions and image names are refreshed every week.
